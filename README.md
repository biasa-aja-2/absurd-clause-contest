One absurd clause, 200 characters at most, hidden deep inside a
terms-of-service agreement: deadpan legal boilerplate that quietly turns
absurd.

> By accepting these terms, you grant the company a perpetual, irrevocable licence to your shadow, including the right to lend it to third parties on cloudy days.

## Why this line

It reads as genuine boilerplate — "perpetual, irrevocable licence" — until
the very last words, when your shadow turns out to be lendable property.
The absurdity is concrete and specific (a shadow, lent to third parties,
on cloudy days), not a vague shrug at the genre, and the joke needs no
setup: one reading is enough, because the punchline ambushes you at the
end. It could sit buried in a real terms of service and only reveal itself
on a careful re-read — exactly what the brief asks for.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

`main.py` (pure Python stdlib, no dependencies) writes the line to
`$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (default `/output/entry.txt`), with a
self-check gate (1–200 characters, single line, not whitespace-only) and a
byte-exact read-back verification.
